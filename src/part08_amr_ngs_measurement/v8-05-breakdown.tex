\section{Why more coverage cannot fix it --- and this part is a theorem}
\markboth{Why coverage fails}{}

\noindent\textbf{\color{ember}The biology question:} \emph{surely if we sequence deeply enough, the
majority of reads gives the right answer?}

\noindent Usually yes. Inside a failed tile-cycle, \textbf{no} --- and the failure is not gradual, it
is total.

\subsection{The 50\,\% wall}

Consensus calling takes a vote. A vote is only trustworthy while most voters are right. The technical
name for the point where an estimator stops working is its \textbf{breakdown point}, and for a
majority vote it is exactly 50\,\%.

\begin{plain}
If most of your witnesses are lying, calling more witnesses does not help --- it just makes the jury
more certain of the wrong verdict. Our four bad tile-cycles are rooms full of witnesses who all tell
the same lie, and the machine gave them all excellent references.
\end{plain}

\subsection{The one-line summary of the mathematics}

\begin{labbox}[title={The mismatch between the model and the world}]
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[font=\footnotesize,

 \node[b=slate] (a) at (-4.2,0)
   {\textbf{\color{slate}What our pipeline assumes}\\[4pt]
    Errors are \textbf{independent} and \textbf{identically distributed} --- every base has roughly
    the same chance of being wrong, independently of every other.\\[4pt]
    {\scriptsize Under this model, averaging is \emph{optimal}. More depth is always better.}};
 \node[b=alert] (b) at (4.2,0)
   {\textbf{\color{alert}What the data shows}\\[4pt]
    Errors are \textbf{structured} --- concentrated in a handful of (tile, cycle) cells, with a
    characteristic substitution spectrum.\\[4pt]
    {\scriptsize Under this model, averaging is \emph{catastrophic} exactly where it matters, and
    more depth is actively harmful.}};
 \draw[-{Stealth[length=7pt]},line width=1.5pt,draw=ember] (-0.55,0)--(0.55,0);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
\vspace{-2pt}
The fix is not more data. The fix is to \textbf{find the structure and remove it} --- which is what
\S10 does, in 50 lines, at no cost.
\end{labbox}
