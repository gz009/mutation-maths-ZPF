\section{Three problems, one shape}
\markboth{Three problems, one shape}{}

You named three things: apparently random mutation, realistic docking and dynamics, and what
AlphaFold\,3 did or did not solve. They look like three fields. They are one mathematical failure,
and the failure has a precise form.

\begin{thesisbox}[title={The thesis, stated once}]
\begin{center}\itshape\large
In every case we have a good theory of the individual object,\\and no tractable theory of the measure
over those objects,\\or of the operator that evolves that measure in time.
\end{center}
\vspace{2pt}
A structure predictor returns a point. Biology needs $(\mu,\mathcal{P}_t)$. A docking program returns
a pose. Biology needs $\Delta G$, which is a ratio of integrals over $\mu$. A mutation model returns a
constant. Biology needs a stochastic field. \textbf{The gap is the same gap three times.}
\end{thesisbox}

\subsection{Why this is not a complaint that ``biology is complicated''}

It is a specific, nameable, provable computational obstruction with a specific mathematical
consequence, and that distinction matters --- because obstructions can be attacked and complexity
cannot.

\textbf{\color{alert}The vague version (not useful)} & \textbf{\color{bio}The precise version (attackable)}\\
\midrule
``Cells are too complex to model.'' &
 $Z$ is \#P-hard, so we substitute $\arg\max_x e^{-\beta U(x)}$ for $\int e^{-\beta U(x)}dx$ ---
 a \emph{mode} for a \emph{measure}.\\
``Docking scoring functions are inaccurate.'' &
 $\Delta G$ is a small difference of large numbers whose dominant terms are entropic, and entropy
 \emph{is} $\ln Z$.\\
``AlphaFold doesn't capture dynamics.'' &
 It estimates a mode of $\mu$. It does not estimate $\mu$, and it does not estimate the transfer
 operator $\mathcal{P}_t$ that governs transitions between the modes.\\
``Mutations are random.'' &
 They are a Cox process --- a Poisson process whose intensity is itself a random field --- with a
 partly quantum-mechanical substrate.\\
