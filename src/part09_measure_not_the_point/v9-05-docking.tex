\section{Docking and dynamics: the error is entropic}
\markboth{Docking}{}

Docking's weakness is not geometry. Poses are usually roughly right. The failure is in the number
attached to the pose, and the reason is arithmetic before it is chemistry.

\noindent And the part that will not cancel is entropic:
\[
\Delta G_{\text{bind}} \;=\; \underbrace{\Delta H}_{\text{computable-ish}} \;-\; T\underbrace{\Delta S}_{\textbf{the problem}}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad S \;=\; -\frac{\partial F}{\partial T},\quad F=-k_BT\ln Z .
\]
\textbf{Entropy \emph{is} $\ln Z$.} So docking's central difficulty is the same villain as §2.

\midrule
\textbf{Desolvation} \newline (ordered water) &
 Displacing structured water from a binding site can supply most of the affinity. The water is
 \emph{part of the receptor}, and it is invisible in most crystal structures. &
 Inhomogeneous solvation theory --- WaterMap, Abel et al., \textit{JACS} \textbf{130}, 2817 (2008).
 \textbf{No rigorous tractable theory of which waters are displaceable, and at what cost.}\\
\textbf{Configurational entropy} &
 Both partners lose conformational freedom on binding. This is an integral over a $3N$-dimensional
 manifold. &
 Free energy perturbation: $\sim$1 kcal/mol accuracy demonstrated (Wang et al., \textit{JACS}
 \textbf{137}, 2695, 2015) --- but \textbf{GPU-days per ligand \emph{pair}}, and only between
 chemically similar ligands, because the alchemical path must be smooth.\\

\subsection{The other wall: fifteen orders of magnitude}

Binding \emph{kinetics} --- residence time $1/k_{\text{off}}$ --- predicts clinical efficacy better
than affinity does for many targets. It is also far harder, because it is a rare-event problem.
