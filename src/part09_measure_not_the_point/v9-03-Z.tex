\section{The villain: why everything reduces to \texorpdfstring{$Z$}{Z}}
\markboth{The partition function}{}

Everything a molecule does at equilibrium follows from one number.

\begin{tier3}[title={Why it is genuinely hard, not merely expensive}]
$Z$ is a $3N$-dimensional integral over a function with an enormous number of sharp, well-separated
maxima. For a modest protein $N\sim10^4$ atoms, so the integral is over $\mathbb{R}^{30{,}000}$.

\textbf{No quadrature scheme reaches that dimension.} The only general approach is Monte Carlo ---
and Monte Carlo converges at $O(n^{-1/2})$ \emph{provided it mixes}. On a rugged landscape with
barriers $\gg k_BT$, it does not mix: escape from a basin takes time $\propto e^{\beta\Delta U}$, so
sampling time is \emph{exponential in the barrier height}.

\subsection{What we do instead --- and what it costs us}

Because $Z$ is out of reach, every practical method quietly replaces the measure with its mode:
\[
\underbrace{\mu(x)=\frac{e^{-\beta U(x)}}{Z}}_{\text{what the molecule \emph{is}}}
\qquad\longrightarrow\qquad
\underbrace{\hat{x}=\arg\max_x\, e^{-\beta U(x)}}_{\text{what we compute}}
\]
This substitution is invisible in the output --- you get a structure, or a pose, or a number --- and
it is exactly wrong whenever the measure is \textbf{multimodal}, which for a functioning protein it
always is.
