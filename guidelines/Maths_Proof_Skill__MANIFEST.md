## 01_Start_Here — 134 pages total

| Book | pp | READ |
|---|---|---|
| `Devlin_Introduction_to_Mathematical_Thinking_2012.pdf` | 117 | **§3.1 "What is a proof?"** → 3.2 contradiction → 3.3 conditionals → 3.4 quantified → **3.5 induction**. Read §2 first if quantifiers are unfamiliar |
| `Thurston_On_Proof_and_Progress_in_Mathematics_1994.pdf` | 17 | **all of it, in one sitting** |

**Devlin is the shortest complete route into proof that exists.** Read §3 this week — it is about
25 pages and it covers every technique in the tutorial notebook.

**Thurston is not a textbook.** A Fields medallist asking *what proof is actually for* and what
mathematicians really do. Read it early; it will change what you think you are doing.

## 02_Main_Reference — your drill manual

| Book | pp | READ |
|---|---|---|
| `Diedrichs_Lovett_Transition_to_Advanced_Mathematics_2022.pdf` | 552 | **Ch. 1** logic & sets (1.1 propositions, 1.3 equivalences, **1.5–1.6 quantifiers**) · **Ch. 2 Arguments and Proofs** (2.1 valid arguments, **2.2 first proof strategies**, **2.3 proof strategies**) · **§4.5 Induction** · §5.2 combinatorial proof |

I grepped Ch. 2 — it names exactly the techniques you need: **direct proof, contrapositive,
contradiction, cases, counterexample, uniqueness**. Hundreds of exercises with answers.

**Use it as a gym, not a novel.** Read a technique, do ten exercises, move on.

## The route

```
week 1   Devlin §3 (25 pp)  +  Thurston (17 pp)
         -> then the tutorial notebook, top to bottom
week 2   Diedrichs & Lovett Ch.1 (logic, quantifiers) + exercises
week 3   D&L Ch.2 (the six techniques) + exercises
week 4   D&L §4.5 induction + notebook exercises 5 and 6
ongoing  Halmos when you want elegance; Tao when you are stuck;
         Radozycki to see proofs written well
```

The tutorial notebook `../Proof_Techniques_01_Sage_10.9_From_Pure_to_Applied.ipynb` is keyed to
Devlin and D&L section numbers throughout — it drives you into these books rather than replacing
them.
